# Language OS Learning Principles

This document is the product constitution for Language OS. It is a decision tool, not a mood board. When a feature, prompt, metric, or interface choice is ambiguous, prefer the choice that best preserves these principles.

## Product premise

Language is learned through meaningful contact. The learner should work with language attached to something they genuinely care about: Korean music and performance culture, Korean media, gaming, technology and AI, philosophy and spirituality, internet culture and slang, or ordinary life.

Generic textbook material is a fallback. Personally meaningful language is the default.

## The core loop

Language OS should repeatedly move through:

**Notice → Guess → Unpack → React → Reuse**

1. **Notice** a phrase in meaningful media or a real expressive need.
2. **Guess** before receiving a complete answer.
3. **Unpack** only as far as needed: vocabulary, structure, sound, nuance, and context.
4. **React** to what the phrase actually means or does.
5. **Reuse** it in speech, writing, thought, or everyday narration.

Explanation is not the end state. The loop must return language to living use.

## Progressive revelation

Start with the language itself. Do not display all support immediately.

Let the learner progressively reveal meaning, segmentation, vocabulary, grammar, nuance, cultural context, and deeper word history or metaphor. Support both a fast intuitive reading and an atom-by-atom analysis, but never require the deepest view for every line.

Productive uncertainty should last long enough for listening, noticing, guessing, recall, and interpretation. Support should arrive before that uncertainty becomes discouraging.

## Korean remains primary

Korean should remain visually and cognitively primary in Korean-learning experiences.

- Do not use romanization.
- Use large, readable Korean and preserve meaningful line or phrase boundaries.
- Treat English as support rather than the center of the experience.
- Prefer short Korean where it is understandable, clear English support where needed, and small alternating units over long explanation blocks.

## Attempts are evidence

The learner should have a chance to record what they hear or think a phrase means before the answer appears. Incorrect guesses are useful evidence, not failure.

Interfaces should preserve meaningful distinctions among:

- immediately understood;
- recognized but uncertain;
- partially understood;
- misunderstood;
- sounds recognized without meaning; and
- completely unknown.

Do not collapse an early attempt into a corrected answer. Preserve the contrast so change remains visible.

## Nuance is part of meaning

A dictionary gloss is often insufficient. Useful analysis may include dictionary and conjugated forms, roots, particles, clause relationships, implied subjects, register, emotional tone, metaphor, near-synonym choice, ordinary-media usage, and whether a line feels poetic, cute, dramatic, blunt, formal, intimate, or unusual.

Depth must earn its place. Reveal it when it changes understanding, listening, or reuse.

## Repetition should evolve

Repeating the same material should change the learner's relationship to it, not repeat the same study action.

A healthy progression is:

1. hear the sounds;
2. recognize familiar pieces;
3. predict meaning;
4. understand structure;
5. hear the phrase as one meaningful unit;
6. recall or reconstruct it; and
7. reuse it independently.

After analysis, the experience should become lighter again. The learner should be able to return to the original media with fewer overlays and more direct understanding.

## Honest comprehension

Comprehension is contextual and unstable. Do not present it as a scientifically precise score.

Use lightweight states and understandable comparisons:

- What was available before study?
- What is available now?
- Which lines collapse when explanations are hidden?
- Which phrases are heard as complete units?
- Which phrases can be recalled or reused?

Progress should be visible without fake precision, points, or false mastery claims.

## Knowledge is a history of evidence

Language knowledge is not a permanent flag. Preserve what the learner encountered, guessed, recognized, missed, recalled, and used, together with when, where, and through which modality it happened.

One appearance proves exposure only. Reading recognition does not imply listening recognition; a learner-created sentence does not prove appropriate usage; a revealed answer does not prove recall. Derive understandable states from the underlying history and make those reasons inspectable.

Normalization must preserve ambiguity. Keep separate meanings and useful forms, retain provenance for AI suggestions, and never silently merge or overwrite learner-confirmed history.

## Small, valuable output

Output is essential, but every session should not become an exhausting test. Select a small number of items with high explanatory or reuse value.

Good output includes rebuilding a phrase, paraphrasing meaning, writing a personal sentence with the same pattern, reacting in simple Korean, narrating a present experience, or comparing nearby grammar choices.

Fragments count. Attempts count. Retrieval matters more than perfect reproduction.

## Dialogue is a preferred direction

Where useful, Language OS should behave like a responsive coach rather than a static textbook. The architecture should leave room for questions such as:

- Why this word?
- What would sound more casual?
- Would someone say this outside a song?
- What did I misunderstand?
- Can I see another sentence with this feeling?
- How can I use this phrase about my life?

Structured screens may guide a vertical slice, but they should not close off conversational exploration.

## Fit real behavior

Build around what the learner already wants to do. A learner may loop a K-pop song in another app while Language OS stays open on an iPad, desktop display, or side monitor. The product should fit beside that behavior instead of forcing a traditional lesson format.

That means calm interfaces, fast controls, minimal interruption, durable local notes, Korean-first listening modes, and analysis that can mostly disappear when it is time to hear the song again.

## Product decision test

Before shipping a learning interaction, ask:

1. Is the language connected to a real interest or expressive need?
2. Does the learner encounter or attempt it before the full answer?
3. Is support revealed in the smallest useful units?
4. Does Korean remain primary and free of romanization?
5. Does the system preserve the learner's initial evidence?
6. Is the depth relevant to meaning, sound, or reuse?
7. Does repetition evolve?
8. Are progress claims honest and understandable?
9. Is there a small, high-value path to output?
10. Can the learner return to real language with less scaffolding?

If several answers are no, reconsider the interaction.
